## Implemented repairs and consequential evidence

The original five finding groups remain repaired: persisted AP/PO authorization, historical purchasing valuation and dated reversals, mixed-entity supplier billing, atomic inbound effect/replay identity, and the invalid pip-wheel Docker filename. Their regression evidence is in `tests/test_enterprise_integrity.py`.

### Commercial document authority — confirmed, high consequence

**Failure:** the supplier-side persistence checks did not cover customer or intercompany invoice facts and lifecycle changes. A writable company aggregate could carry invoice removal, altered prices, overpayments or backdated receipts independently of the action endpoint. This could misstate receivables, cash, sales, tax and intercompany balances across departments.

**Repair:** `engine/scp/companies/commercial.py:12` replays new billing, payments and cancellations against domain rules; retains existing facts/payments; checks shipped sales ownership/prices and credit blocks; and protects shipped freight and settled carrier bills. `companies/transactions.py` applies these checks to whole saves, patches, merges and restores. Duplicate commercial identifiers and malformed posted records are rejected. New invoices record the authenticated entrant.

**Validation:** `tests/test_commercial_integrity.py` attacks removal, repricing, overpayment, pre-invoice payment and duplicate identifiers through whole/patch/merge routes. It checks forged new billing and intercompany prices, shipment credit blocks, and rollback of refused writes. Ordinary order-to-cash and transport tests remain required. The guard does not establish complete authority over every manufacturing/warehouse exception or every draft object in the aggregate.

### Historical cancellations and transfer prices — confirmed, high consequence

**Failure:** sales/intercompany cancellation flags removed documents from earlier reports. Transfer prices could also follow editable rules after goods were posted. These effects could restate closed periods and change entity inventory or intercompany margin without a dated posting.

**Repair:** sales and intercompany cancellations have dates, preserve original vouchers and post balanced reversals on the cancellation date. Payment/cancellation chronology is enforced. Rebilling before a future cancellation is refused. Customer invoices are separated by posting entity and retain that entity. Transfer material documents retain their posting price; billing uses that price. Legacy snapshots use the saved pre-edit basis. Relevant transfer/entity master changes participate in master-data approval.

**Evidence:** `sales/__init__.py:865`, `sales/__init__.py:950`, `intercompany/__init__.py`, `accounting/__init__.py:474`, `actuals/transfer_price.py:164`, `actuals/documents.py`, `actuals/valuation.py`, and the associated commercial integrity tests. Tests compare the entire earlier accounting report before/after a later cancellation, reconcile both entity reversals, and change a transfer rule from 12 to 20 after 50 units shipped: the posted/billed net remains 600.

**Limit:** absent historical rates, entrants or cancellation dates cannot be reconstructed from current source. A snapshot taken from a saved legacy document preserves that known basis against further edits; it does not prove that the earlier imported facts were correct. Initial company creation and historic migration still require independent opening-data approval.

### Business grants and separation of duties — confirmed implementation; configuration required

**Failure:** owner/planner/viewer membership and place/family scope did not express a departmental authorization matrix. Recording a releaser's identity alone did not establish that a designated controller or treasury person performed the operation.

**Repair:** `companies/controls.py:23` enforces named grants at the persistence boundary for master data, planning, purchasing, sales, manufacturing, warehouse, transport, billing, payments, invoice release, credit release, period close and inventory adjustment. Combined entry/payment or entry/release requires both grants. Scrap, openings, adjustments and unplanned receipts require inventory-adjustment authority. Once the matrix is configured, omitted grants deny the operation, including for owners and integration identities. Existing membership/place/family checks still apply. Owners administer policy; new policy cannot authorize the same write retroactively. Optional payment separation requires a different person from the recorded entrant and releaser. A named controller can close a period; closing checks reconciliation and suspense. Replacement cannot reopen it.

**Validation:** tests deny an ungranted owner's billing, a buyer's payment and a planner's policy escalation; permit a separately granted treasury actor; reject self-payment even when one actor has both grants; and check controller close/reopen behavior. Legacy invoices with unknown entrants are refused under payment separation until independently reviewed migration evidence exists.

**Deployment requirement:** the empty matrix intentionally preserves legacy membership behavior. It is not enterprise segregation of duties. Configure `finance.control_roles`, enable `finance.separate_payments`, obtain business-owner approval of the matrix and test each actual identity/key. Keys have the identity `<lowercase-key-id>.<lowercase-company-id>@keys.invalid`; their company membership must also remain valid. Policy administration, privileged override, identity lifecycle and retention require independent governance review. No complete IAM/SCIM or statutory SoD certification is claimed.

### Foreign settlement, inventory policy and unsupported precision — confirmed controls; finance scope restricted

**Failure:** supplier settlement reused the invoice exchange rate for bank effects. A different settlement rate did not generate realized exchange gain/loss. Direct stock writes could also bypass the saved negative-stock policy. The monetary model uses floats and two-decimal amounts regardless of currency.

**Repair:** payments retain the settlement rate; AP clears at the original rate, bank posts at the settlement rate, and realized gain/loss is explicit. Missing foreign posting rates are refused. Direct material writes use the saved negative-stock policy and opening quantity, including backdated stock effects. Known currencies requiring zero, three or four decimal places are rejected at financial posting/accounting/export boundaries, preventing silent two-decimal ledger output.

**Validation:** for a 1,000 USD liability at INR 80/USD, tests clear AP by 80,000 and post bank at 85,000 with 5,000 exchange loss, or bank at 75,000 with 5,000 exchange gain; suspense remains zero and later planning FX edits preserve reports. Stock tests attempt to inflate planning on-hand and relax the policy in the same forged write. JPY/KWD/CLF tests reject financial saves and ledger exports.

**Still required:** decimal monetary arithmetic, approved rounding rules, a complete currency master/minor-unit implementation, foreign bank-account semantics, explicit standard-cost revaluation and full close/reopen governance. The precision guard contains an unsupported capability; it does not implement it. The raw probe `verification/erp-release-currency-probe.json` demonstrates the underlying limitation: an unguarded model amount of 1.234 KWD becomes 1.23, and the half-cent input 0.7 × 0.35 is rounded by the existing float/two-decimal policy. The probe does not establish which tie-breaking policy a particular customer has approved; that acceptance rule is still missing. Do not claim broad multinational finance support.

### Outbound recovery and process leadership — confirmed, high/medium consequence

**Failure:** an interrupted SMTP send had no persisted MIME/attachments for recovery; its `sending` state was outside the declared response enum. Closing a PostgreSQL connection could return before its scheduler leadership locks were released.

**Repair:** `connect/outbox.py:155` stores the complete MIME before SMTP, retaining attachments and Message-ID. Sending/unknown states are representable. A verified human owner can recover an interrupted/failed entry through `POST /api/companies/{cid}/mail/{seq}/retry`, after checking provider outcome and explicitly acknowledging possible duplication. A durable original-to-retry receipt makes a repeated recovery request idempotent. Active sends are not retried within ten minutes. Current recipient authorization and daily limits still apply. `postgres.py:113` explicitly releases session locks before graceful close.

**Validation:** a local sink simulates worker death after SMTP acceptance but before recording the outcome. The recovered message retains its exact payload and Message-ID; repeating the recovery request causes no extra send. Preparation failures record failure before SMTP. PostgreSQL backup/restore retains MIME bytes and retry identity. Multi-process tests verify leadership handoff and disconnected-session behavior.

**Limit:** SMTP is not an exactly-once protocol. The simulated crash can already have delivered the first message. Stable Message-ID assists reconciliation; provider deduplication is not assumed. Real mail/ERP/WMS/bank certification, reconciliation ownership, monitoring/alerts and infrastructure-failure recovery remain open.

### Warehouse facts and scoped authority — confirmed, high consequence

**Affected processes:** goods receipt, quality/status transfer, physical inventory, serial custody and departmental master-data maintenance. Baseline counterexamples at `1f7a041` persisted an unbalanced status movement, scrap during a blocked physical count and a duplicate serial receipt. The same five-case probe subsequently verified all five original writes as HTTP 200/revision 2 and all repaired writes as HTTP 403/revision 1 (`reproduction/erp-warehouse-probe-before.json` and `...-after.json` in the package). Another accepted write changed a foreign product's family into the actor's allowed family and then edited it. The evidence files retain the accepted revision; these were executable failures, not inferred omissions.

**Failure mechanism and consequence:** raw company writes did not replay these warehouse invariants, and product-family authorization used proposed ownership for both sides of a write. This could create stock, invalidate a frozen count, duplicate serial custody or widen a user's own write scope. High severity follows from direct effects on inventory, traceability and authorization inside the implemented warehouse process.

**Smallest defensible repair:** `companies/materials.py:20` checks status quantity conservation; retains existing same-day movement order; reconstructs physical count books against saved state, verifies exact posted differences and retains completed/cancelled documents; enforces count blocks and serial quantity, uniqueness, place/type custody and chronology. Disabling serial control while stock remains is refused. `companies/rights.py:124` checks original and proposed family ownership separately; count items must all be inside the assigned plants/families. `companies/controls.py:23` separates inventory adjustment from ordinary warehouse work and requires payments/releases even when combined with invoice entry in one save.

**Reproduction/validation:** `tests/test_material_integrity.py` exercises whole/patch/merge attacks, forged count books/deltas, counted stock 100 to 95, cancellation, duplicate/missing/backdated/wrong-place serials and valid atomic transfers. `tests/test_family_scope_integrity.py` denies cross-family takeover and mixed-plant counts while permitting an authorized scoped count. `tests/test_company_commands.py::test_combined_invoice_entry_and_payment_requires_both_department_grants` rejects a billing-only actor's combined paid invoice. These controls contain the demonstrated defects. Independent warehouse approval of serial migration, quality, bin/wave exceptions and physical counts remains required.

### Authoritative operations and recovery receipts — confirmed implementation

`POST /api/companies/{cid}/commands` now reads the saved company and executes typed purchasing, sales, stock, transport or intercompany actions. It requires an explicit business date, expected revision and stable command ID. A caller cannot replace the dataset or inject reserved/unknown parameters. `companies/commands.py:120` commits effects, revision/audit and the relational receipt (`companies/store.py:85`) in one transaction. A held required master change aborts the entire command. The operation preserves unrelated raw schema shape, so merely parsing defaults does not demand unrelated master-data grants.

A repeat of the same ID/date/business parameters returns the original result plus the **current** saved state; changing its input returns 409. Distinct commands based on one revision cannot both post. `GET .../commands?id=...` reconciles a lost response and retains actor, original parameters, date, result, hash, revision and build commit. Account export and database backup/migration retain receipts. See `OPERATIONS.md` for the concrete request and configuration.

**Validation:** command tests inject a receipt-write failure and require company/revision/audit/receipt rollback, then a successful retry. Concurrent identical calls produce one payment. A goods-receipt replay after intervening scrap preserves the later stock level. PostgreSQL tests use separate store connections and repeat the operation after backup/restore, with one bank effect and one receipt. A minimal document traverses receiving, billing and separate treasury actors with only their own grants. This establishes the new boundary's tested behavior; it does not certify every action adapter's entire business exception matrix or migrate every GUI screen. Document and posting dates still share the existing operation-date model.

## Architecture and measured scale

**Inferred architectural judgment:** a modular monolith, pure planning functions, typed network models and immutable scenario revisions are defensible foundations. This is a design assessment from inspected code and tests, not a reference-system certification. Microservices or replacing every JSON object with a table would not establish business correctness by themselves.

The consequential boundary is between editable scenario state and authoritative operational facts. Live company state remains one JSON aggregate; critical invariants depend on complete application enforcement across all mutation routes. The new authoritative command boundary and relational command receipts strengthen that design without replacing the monolith. Separating operational posting records with tenant/document constraints remains further work; the new receipt table is not a complete relational ledger. Planning should consume explicit input revisions. Existing browser preview/save flows still use shared persistence guards; they have not all been migrated to commands.

The PostgreSQL adapter uses a shared connection and advisory lock; `StoreLock` also serializes threads. Removing the lock alone would make nested transactions and read-check-write operations unsafe. A safe concurrency redesign needs independently scoped connections/transactions, company-level serialization and retained CAS/atomic replay semantics, with separate leadership locks for global jobs.

Profiling identified unnecessary audit work, not just database waiting: a simple 3 MB document edit spent 0.879 of 0.992 seconds rebuilding unchanged audit collections, including roughly 110,000 JSON dumps. `versions/diff.py` now skips unchanged collections and avoids sorting singleton groups. Differential comparison against the preceding implementation matched all 500 seeded cases, including duplicate/missing identifiers, reordering and nested changes; existing revision/merge tests also pass.
